<h2>Beyond the Horizon: Daily Riffs from the Gen AI Songbook</h2>

<h3>TOP THEME OF THE DAY</h3>
<p>The AI investment story is increasingly shifting from model excitement to execution capacity: data centers, storage, power, fuel cells, permitting, and secure frontier-model operations. For advisor conversations, the key point is that AI opportunity now extends beyond software leaders into infrastructure and energy ecosystems, while risks are rising around capital intensity, local opposition, cybersecurity, and regulatory bottlenecks.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>BofA projected data center spending will more than double to $2.1 trillion by 2028:</strong> Reuters reported that BofA sees data center spending rising sharply as AI demand drives a large physical infrastructure buildout. The investment read-through is that AI capex remains a multi-year theme, but one that requires discipline around power availability, utilization, financing costs, and valuation expectations across the supply chain. (Source: Reuters)</li>
<li><strong>OpenAI said it is scaling online storage to serve more than 1 billion ChatGPT users:</strong> The company’s update points to a quantified adoption inflection, showing that consumer AI usage has reached a scale where storage, reliability, and backend architecture become strategic differentiators. For advisors, this supports the view that monetization and infrastructure demand can follow usage intensity, but it also highlights rising operating complexity and cost. (Source: OpenAI)</li>
<li><strong>AI data centers are boosting fuel-cell demand:</strong> Reuters reported that power needs from AI data centers are contributing to increased interest in fuel cells as alternative or supplemental energy sources. This broadens the AI infrastructure conversation beyond chips and servers to onsite power, grid resiliency, and energy systems that may benefit if traditional utility interconnection timelines remain slow. (Source: Reuters)</li>
<li><strong>The FCC restricted imports of certain foreign-made advanced robots:</strong> The Robot Report highlighted a webinar on how the FCC ruling affects automation buyers, including implications for mobile and legged robots. The development matters because physical AI adoption is becoming tied to cybersecurity, supply-chain sourcing, and regulatory compliance, not only labor savings or warehouse productivity. (Source: The Robot Report)</li>
</ul>

<h3>WHAT TO WATCH</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Data center capex durability:</strong> Watch whether hyperscalers and large enterprises maintain elevated AI infrastructure spending as financing costs, utilization rates, and power constraints become more visible.</li>
<li><strong>Power procurement and onsite generation:</strong> Track fuel cells, grid interconnection queues, power purchase agreements, and backup generation as leading indicators of where AI capacity can actually come online.</li>
<li><strong>Local permitting risk:</strong> Monitor state and municipal data center rules, moratoriums, tax negotiations, and water or land-use disputes that could delay projects.</li>
<li><strong>Enterprise agent conversion:</strong> Look for evidence that AI agents are moving from demos to paid, governed workflow deployments with measurable productivity or cost outcomes.</li>
</ul>

<h3>ADVISOR / WHOLESALER SOUNDBITES</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>AI is becoming an infrastructure story:</strong> The next phase is not just better models; it is power, storage, networking, cooling, permitting, and secure operations.</li>
<li><strong>Usage scale matters:</strong> A billion-user AI product can create real infrastructure demand, but it also raises questions about cost, reliability, and monetization.</li>
<li><strong>Power is a portfolio issue:</strong> AI data centers are pulling energy technologies into the conversation, from utilities and grid equipment to onsite generation such as fuel cells.</li>
<li><strong>Regulation is moving closer to the physical layer:</strong> Data centers and advanced robots are now facing local, national, and cybersecurity-driven policy scrutiny.</li>
<li><strong>Enterprise AI needs workflows, not just chatbots:</strong> The most durable business value may come when AI agents are embedded into governed processes that companies already rely on.</li>
</ul>
