<h3>TOP THEME OF THE DAY</h3>
<p>Today’s strongest signal is that the AI cycle is moving from model excitement toward production infrastructure, governed agent workflows, and the physical capacity needed to support them. For advisors, the key conversation is no longer just which model is best, but how capital, cloud platforms, power, manufacturing automation, and policy constraints shape the pace and profitability of AI adoption.</p>

<h3>TOP STORIES</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>DayOne filed for a US IPO amid the AI data center rush:</strong> Singapore-based DayOne is seeking a US listing as investor appetite remains focused on funding data center capacity tied to AI demand. The filing reinforces that AI infrastructure remains a major capital-markets channel, while also raising diligence questions around power access, utilization, customer concentration, and valuation discipline. (Source: Bloomberg.com)</li>
<li><strong>AWS added OpenAI-powered managed agents inside Bedrock:</strong> AWS announced a public preview of Amazon Bedrock Managed Agents powered by OpenAI, using a customized version of OpenAI’s Agents API designed to operate natively in AWS. This is a Platform Convergence and Enterprise Production Readiness signal: leading clouds are trying to make agentic AI deployable within existing security, data, and governance environments rather than as standalone experiments. (Source: AWS News Blog)</li>
<li><strong>ServiceNow launched AI Workflow Factory:</strong> ServiceNow introduced AI Workflow Factory to turn workflow improvement into a continuous agentic AI-powered loop. The investment read-through is that enterprise AI monetization may increasingly come from workflow orchestration and process redesign, not only from model access or chatbot interfaces. (Source: ServiceNow)</li>
<li><strong>Teradyne invested in Bright Machines to automate AI infrastructure manufacturing:</strong> Teradyne made a strategic investment in Bright Machines and announced a collaboration combining robotics, test technologies, and software-defined manufacturing for AI and data center infrastructure production. This broadens the AI investment lens into factory automation and testing capacity, where physical throughput can become a constraint on digital infrastructure expansion. (Source: The Robot Report)</li>
</ul>

<h3>WHAT TO WATCH</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>AI infrastructure IPO quality:</strong> Monitor whether data center listings show contracted demand, credible power access, disciplined leverage, and realistic utilization assumptions.</li>
<li><strong>Agent governance adoption:</strong> Watch whether enterprises move from pilots to controlled production use of agents with permissions, budgets, audit trails, and human escalation paths.</li>
<li><strong>Local permitting risk:</strong> Track whether more municipalities pursue data center moratoriums, bans, or stricter power and water reviews that could delay AI capacity additions.</li>
<li><strong>Open-model competition:</strong> Watch benchmark credibility, enterprise licensing terms, and cloud distribution partnerships as open-weight models compete with closed frontier systems.</li>
</ul>

<h3>ADVISOR / WHOLESALER SOUNDBITES</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>AI is becoming an infrastructure cycle, not just a software cycle:</strong> Data centers, power, manufacturing automation, and permitting are now central to the investment story.</li>
<li><strong>Enterprises want agents, but they also want guardrails:</strong> The next phase of adoption depends on governance, spending controls, security, and workflow integration.</li>
<li><strong>Cloud platforms are becoming the control point for enterprise AI:</strong> AWS adding OpenAI-powered managed agents shows that distribution and trust may matter as much as model performance.</li>
<li><strong>Local opposition is an underappreciated AI bottleneck:</strong> Even with strong demand, data center growth can be slowed by community, land, power, and environmental constraints.</li>
<li><strong>Physical AI is moving through the picks-and-shovels layer first:</strong> Robotics software, test systems, and automated manufacturing may benefit before humanoid or fully autonomous robotics reach broad deployment.</li>
</ul>
