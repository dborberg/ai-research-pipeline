<h3>WHAT TO WATCH</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Power-flexible data-center evidence:</strong> Watch for named partners, project locations, capacity figures, financing structures, and customer commitments that separate concept announcements from investable infrastructure buildouts.</li>
  <li><strong>Anthropic IPO-readiness signals:</strong> Monitor whether any new model release comes with measurable capability gains, enterprise distribution, safety documentation, pricing clarity, and customer traction.</li>
  <li><strong>Local data-center politics:</strong> Track moratoriums, public hearings, utility interconnection delays, and water or land-use restrictions as leading indicators of AI infrastructure friction.</li>
  <li><strong>AI governance standard-setting:</strong> Watch whether frontier-lab safety bodies gain regulator recognition, publish enforceable standards, or become viewed as insufficient self-policing.</li>
</ul>

<h3>ADVISOR / WHOLESALER SOUNDBITES</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>AI is becoming a power story:</strong> The next phase is not just who has the best model, but who can secure enough reliable energy and data-center capacity to run it.</li>
  <li><strong>Infrastructure exposure still requires selectivity:</strong> A strong AI theme does not automatically make every power, data-center, or fuel-cell company equally positioned.</li>
  <li><strong>Frontier-model IPOs will test market discipline:</strong> Investors may reward technical leadership, but public-market scrutiny will focus on revenue quality, margins, customer concentration, and safety risk.</li>
  <li><strong>Governance is moving from theory to operating cost:</strong> As regulators and industry groups build AI oversight structures, compliance and security may become core parts of enterprise AI budgets.</li>
  <li><strong>For clients, separate adoption from hype:</strong> The best AI conversations should ask where companies are seeing real usage, measurable productivity, and bottleneck relief, not just whether they mention AI.</li>
</ul>
