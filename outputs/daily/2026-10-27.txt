<h2>Beyond the Horizon: Daily Riffs from the Gen AI Songbook</h2>
<p><strong>October 27, 2026</strong></p>

<h3>TOP THEME OF THE DAY</h3>
<p>AI investment momentum is broadening from models and chips into the physical systems needed to deploy intelligence at scale: power, edge computing, industrial automation, and autonomous equipment. For advisor conversations, the key point is that the AI cycle is becoming less purely software-led and more dependent on execution in infrastructure, energy access, factory floors, and capital-intensive real-world deployment.</p>

<h3>TOP STORIES</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bedrock Robotics funding:</strong> Bedrock Robotics raised $600 million in Series C funding at a $4.1 billion valuation, bringing total funding to nearly $1 billion as it scales autonomous heavy equipment for construction sites. The deal is a major physical-AI capital-markets signal and highlights investor interest in applying autonomy to labor-constrained, asset-heavy industries beyond data centers and software workflows. (Source: The Robot Report)</li>
<li><strong>AI power constraint moves to microgrids:</strong> Reuters reported that the AI scaling race is accelerating investment in microgrid technology as data center operators look for more reliable and flexible power supply. The read-through is that AI infrastructure spending is increasingly tied to grid resilience, distributed power, controls, and energy equipment rather than compute hardware alone. (Source: Reuters)</li>
<li><strong>AI chip demand lifts a national macro print:</strong> South Korea’s third-quarter GDP beat forecasts as the AI chip boom boosted exports and investment. The report shows that AI infrastructure demand is already large enough to influence macro outcomes in semiconductor-linked economies, while also reminding advisors that the cycle has exposure to export demand, memory pricing, and capex volatility. (Source: Reuters)</li>
<li><strong>Industrial edge AI consolidation:</strong> OMRON agreed to acquire 100% of OnLogic for $675 million to combine industrial automation, control, robotics, safety, and rugged edge-computing capabilities. The transaction points to platform convergence in manufacturing, where AI value may depend on running models close to machines, sensors, and safety-critical workflows. (Source: The Robot Report)</li>
</ul>
