2. Physical AI and workflow automation are extending AI adoption into construction, energy, manufacturing, and industrial operations, with robotics, private wireless, edge orchestration, and fleet management becoming necessary infrastructure for productivity gains outside software-only markets.
3. Platform convergence is accelerating as AI systems combine models, proprietary data streams, customer-controlled deployment environments, agentic context windows, and vertical APIs, creating defensible distribution advantages for firms that control both data access and workflow integration.

Second-Order Effects:
1. AI infrastructure bottlenecks may redistribute profit pools toward utilities, grid equipment suppliers, gas infrastructure owners, cooling providers, insurers, construction automation firms, and permitting specialists, while raising capital intensity and execution risk for model platforms.

3.
