(Sources: WPLN News; Nashville Scene; news8000.com)

3. China’s post-July 15 AI rules reportedly pushed Doubao and Qwen agents offline while allowing elderly care robots to move forward. The business relevance is that governance can decide which AI products reach market, especially for higher-autonomy agents and physical AI systems. (Source: 36Kr)

AI PRACTICE TIP OF THE WEEK
What: Use AI to create a “first onboarding meeting control sheet” that turns a new client’s intake notes into the decisions, missing documents, and handoffs you need to resolve before implementation starts.

Why: Onboarding often stalls because small gaps are discovered too late: beneficiary details, outside account access, tax documents, cost basis, estate documents, employer plan rules, or risk tolerance conflicts. This week’s AI theme is governed workflows, and the same idea applies here: use AI to make the onboarding meeting more controlled, sequenced, and complete.

How to:
1. Paste anonymized intake notes, stated goals, known accounts, and documents received.
2. Ask AI to identify missing items, decision points, dependencies, and questions for the meeting.
3. Convert the output into a one-page internal prep sheet for the advisor and service team.

Copy prompt: Act as an experienced financial advisor preparing for a first client onboarding meeting. Using the anonymized intake information below, create a one-page internal control sheet with: 1) the top items to confirm in the meeting, 2) missing documents or data, 3) decisions the client needs to make, 4) dependencies that could delay implementation, 5) service team follow-ups, and 6) three plain-English questions I should ask to uncover hidden complexity. Do not make recommendations. Intake information: [paste anonymized notes].

Guardrail: Do not enter names, account numbers, Social Security numbers, or other sensitive client data. Treat the output as a prep aid only; verify all facts and keep recommendations within your firm’s approved planning and compliance process.
