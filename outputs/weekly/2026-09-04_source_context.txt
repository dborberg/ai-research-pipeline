  TITLE: READY or Not: Reliable Enterprise Agent Deployment
  SOURCE: cs.AI updates on arXiv.org
  SUMMARY: The article introduces READY, a framework for determining whether AI agents are suitable for enterprise deployment based on reliability targets, required human oversight, and operating cost. In a clinical-audit case study covering 16 agent systems and 750 cases, READY found that agents with nearly identical autonomous accuracy could require materially different levels of human review to meet the same reliability threshold.

Emerging Trends:
1. AI infrastructure is becoming a constrained industrial buildout rather than a pure semiconductor story, with power access, cooling, land, incentives, and permitting emerging as investable bottlenecks as data-center demand collides with grid and political limits.
2. The AI stack is consolidating around vertically integrated platforms that connect models, developer ecosystems, hardware, and enterprise deployment tooling, suggesting distribution control and workflow ownership may matter as much as model quality.
3. Agentic AI is moving from demos into production-grade vertical workflows across cybersecurity, commerce, coding, logistics, agriculture, and robotics, with efficiency gains in tool use and token consumption improving unit economics for enterprise deployment.

Converging Signals:
1. Multiple clusters point to data-center growth being gated by real-world infrastructure friction: Texas power restrictions, moratorium calls, large incentives to secure AI facilities, national AI budget commitments, and rising demand for power and cooling suppliers.
2. Enterprise agent adoption is converging around safety, reliability, and orchestration layers, shown by agentic cybersecurity partnerships, commerce-agent blueprints, physical-device standards, and reliable enterprise deployment frameworks.
3. Physical AI is broadening from robotics pilots into operating systems for real-world industries, with signals across last-mile delivery optimization, farm operations platforms, robot adoption marketplaces, autonomous equipment R&D, and physical-AI data/model acquisitions.

Second-Order Effects:
1. AI profit pools are broadening beyond the largest model and cloud platforms toward infrastructure suppliers, grid-adjacent assets, cooling vendors, data-center developers, robotics integrators, enterprise orchestration software, and specialized data/model providers.
2. Access to power, permits, and trusted deployment standards is becoming a strategic moat, likely separating AI projects with durable capacity and governance readiness from speculative 'ghost demand' that may be delayed, repriced, or canceled.
3. Professional and operational workflows are shifting from human-operated software tools to AI-mediated execution systems, pressuring seat-based software and labor-services models while creating demand for outcome-based platforms, agent supervision, security, and compliance layers.
